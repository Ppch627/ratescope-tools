Get paid on time

A late fee works only when it is agreed before the work starts

Payment terms should be visible in the proposal, contract, and invoice. State when payment is due, how it should be made, what happens after the due date, and who to contact if there is a billing problem.

A practical overdue-invoice sequence

  1. Before the due date: confirm receipt and ask whether the invoice has entered the client's payment system.
  2. One to three days late: send a short reminder with the invoice attached and repeat the payment date.
  3. After the grace period: reference the agreed term, state the amount now due, and request a payment date in writing.
  4. If it remains unpaid: pause non-essential new work, escalate to the correct decision-maker, and consider formal collection options appropriate to the amount and jurisdiction.
Keep communication factual and professional. Threats, abusive messages, public pressure, or unauthorized charges can damage your position and may create legal risk.

Reduce late payments before they happen

  • Take a deposit before committing delivery capacity.
  • Use milestones for longer projects instead of one final invoice.
  • Put the legal business name, tax details, purchase-order reference, and payment instructions on every invoice.
  • Ask new clients how their accounts-payable process works before you start.
  • Do not deliver final files or transfer rights beyond what the contract permits until payment is complete.

Questions about overdue invoices

Can I charge interest if the contract is silent?

Possibly, depending on local law, but never assume. Check the applicable rules and get professional advice before adding a charge that was not agreed.

Should late fees compound?

This calculator uses simple interest. Compounding or fixed late charges may have different legal treatment. Your contract should state the method clearly.

What if the client disputes the invoice?

Pause the late-fee countdown for the disputed portion if your agreement requires it, and resolve the factual dispute in writing before escalating.
