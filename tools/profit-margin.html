Know the difference

Margin is not markup

Margin compares profit with revenue. Markup compares profit with cost. A 50% markup is only a 33.3% margin, which is why a price can look generous while leaving less profit than expected.

  • Profit: revenue minus all costs included in the calculation.
  • Margin: profit divided by revenue, expressed as a percentage.
  • Markup: profit divided by cost, expressed as a percentage.
  • Break-even revenue: the revenue required to cover entered costs after payment fees.

Which costs belong here?

Direct delivery costs are easy to connect to the work. Overhead is shared across the business and must be allocated using a consistent method such as hours worked, revenue share, or an equal monthly amount. If a cost is excluded, the margin shown will be higher than the real result.

Do not compare margins across different business models without checking what each owner included. A software product and a bespoke agency project carry very different cost structures.

How to improve margin without overworking

  1. Raise prices where demand and outcomes justify it.
  2. Reduce expensive delivery inputs or replace low-value work with repeatable templates.
  3. Improve estimates so revisions and admin are paid for.
  4. Move repeatable services into productized packages with a defined scope.
  5. Track actual hours and costs after delivery, then update the next quote.

Questions about freelance margin

What is a good freelance profit margin?

It depends on risk, utilization, and business model. A solo service business may need a higher margin than a productized agency because revenue is less predictable and time cannot be scaled easily.

Should owner pay be a cost?

For decision-making, it is useful to separate market-rate owner pay from profit. If you do not include it, a positive margin may simply be hidden compensation for your time.

Why is my break-even revenue higher than my costs?

Payment processing takes a percentage of revenue. The business must collect enough revenue so the amount remaining after the fee covers all other costs.
